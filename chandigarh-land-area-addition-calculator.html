1. Comprehensive Guide to UT Chandigarh Land Measurement System

Managing real estate, ancestral land parcels, and commercial spaces in the Union Territory of Chandigarh demands absolute accuracy and complete adherence to administrative standards. As a planned city designed by Le Corbusier, Chandigarh maintains a distinct dual ecosystem of land records. In urban sectors (Sectors 1 to 60+), land is predominantly measured and transferred in Square Yards (Gaj), Square Feet, or Kanal/Marla system for residential bungalows. Conversely, in the peripheral villages and suburban zones administered under the UT Revenue Department—such as Manimajra, Burail, Attawa, Hallomajra, Behlana, Maloya, Dadu Majra, Khuda Alisher, Dhanas, and Sarangpur—land records strictly utilize the traditional Kanal, Marla, and Sarsai metrics derived from the historic Punjab Land Revenue Act.

When dealing with property inheritances (Intiqal/Mutation), title deeds (Sale Deeds), partitions (Takseem), or consolidation of multiple Jamabandi/Fard records, aggregating plot sizes using ordinary mobile calculators results in critical mathematical errors. Standard decimal calculators operate on base-10 mathematics. However, the land measurement framework in Chandigarh relies on a base-20 matrix (20 Marlas = 1 Kanal) and base-9 sub-matrix (9 Sarsai = 1 Marla). 4. Legal & Historical Context of Chandigarh Land Laws (2026 Framework)

Land administration in the Union Territory of Chandigarh is governed by a unique blend of urban regulations and legacy agrarian laws. Following the reorganization of Punjab in 1966, Chandigarh was designated as a Union Territory, adopting the Punjab Land Revenue Act, 1887 for its rural boundaries, alongside the Capital of Punjab (Development and Regulation) Act, 1952 for urban sector administration.

In 2026, the Chandigarh Administration and the Estate Office continue to enforce strict land use and record-keeping mandates. Transactions involving agricultural land within UT villages require accurate entry into the **Fard Jamabandi** (Record of Rights) kept by the Halqa Patwari. Any mismatch between physical plot boundaries, sale deed figures, and revenue department entries can freeze land mutation, delay bank loan approvals, or trigger litigation before the Sub-Divisional Magistrate (SDM) or Land Acquisition Collector (LAC).

Furthermore, understanding the conversion between regional units and modern metric standards is essential. For instance, 1 Marla in Chandigarh is strictly standardized to 30.25 Square Yards (272.25 Square Feet). This contrasts with certain central Indian states where Marla or Bigha metrics carry fluctuating regional definitions. ३. वास्तविक गणना उदाहरण (Base-20 एवं Base-9 कैरी नियम)

यह सॉफ्टवेयर टूल बैकएंड पर गणना को किस प्रकार संभालता है, इसे मनीमाजरा (चंडीगढ़) के एक व्यावहारिक उदाहरण से समझें:

  • जमीन का टुकड़ा १: 3 कनाल, 15 मरला और 7 सरसाई
  • जमीन का टुकड़ा २: 2 कनाल, 12 मरला और 5 सरसाई

स्टेप-बाय-स्टेप जोड़ की प्रक्रिया:

  1. सरसाई इकाइयों का जोड़: 7 सरसाई + 5 सरसाई = 12 सरसाई। चूंकि 9 सरसाई = 1 मरला होता है, अतः 12 - 9 = 3 सरसाई शेष बचेगा और 1 मरला कैरी (हासिल) होकर मरला कॉलम में जुड़ जाएगा।
  2. मरला इकाइयों का जोड़: 15 मरला + 12 मरला + 1 मरला (हासिल का) = 28 मरला। चूंकि 20 मरला = 1 कनाल होता है, अतः 28 - 20 = 8 मरला शेष बचेगा और 1 कनाल मुख्य कनाल के हिस्से में जुड़ जाएगा।
  3. कनाल इकाइयों का जोड़: 3 कनाल + 2 कनाल + 1 कनाल (हासिल का) = 6 कनाल।
  4. अंतिम समेकित परिणाम: कैलकुलेटर सटीक परिणाम 6 कनाल, 8 मरला और 3 सरसाई प्रदर्शित करेगा। साथ ही इसे 3,872 वर्ग गज और 34,848 वर्ग फुट में बदल देगा। साधारण कैलकुलेटर इसे गलत तरीके से 5.27 कनाल दिखाता जो कानूनी दस्तावेजों में अमान्य होता।
